# Fewer, user-set reminders beat clever nagging

The best task apps of 2025-2026 make per-task reminders **opt-in, attached to a chosen time, and quiet by default**. Todoist is the main exception: it adds an automatic reminder to any task with a time. Things goes furthest the other way. Its reminder is a nudge to *start* a task, deadlines get no reminder at all, and its makers advise using reminders "sparingly". Persistent nagging (Due's auto-snooze, TickTick's Constant Reminder, the iOS 26 AlarmKit alarms) is always a separate, explicit opt-in, and most of it is native-only, so a PWA can't copy it. Date-only tasks usually get either one 9:00 AM alert (Apple) or no push at all, with the daily list doing the work instead (Things, Any.do's single morning push). Overdue tasks are handled by showing them and offering to reschedule, never by re-notifying. The research is real but thinner than the marketing suggests. Notifications measurably raise inattention, batching to about three predictable deliveries a day improves well-being, and self-criticism predicts more procrastination. No trial isolates what push reminders do to task-starting in adults with ADHD. On the technical side, scheduled local notifications on the web are dead, so a reminder that fires while the app is closed needs **server-sent Web Push**. On iPhone that only works for Home Screen web apps, and iPhone notifications have no action buttons. For Presense this points to two phases. First, fix the broken ritual notification and the permission flow now. Then build opt-in, start-oriented, per-task Web Push reminders on pg_cron and a Deno Edge Function, and retire the dead countdown and overdue notification columns.

## Most leading apps attach reminders to "when to start", not to the deadline

The mainstream apps split into two models. **Todoist adds an "Automatic" reminder whenever a task has both a date and a time.** You can set the offset or switch it off under Settings > Reminders, and reminders go out by desktop, mobile or email ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-reminders-9PezfU)). TickTick has a global "Default Reminder" setting ([TickTick](https://help.ticktick.com/articles/7055782395743567872)). Google Tasks has no reminder field at all: "to enable notifications for a task, add both a specific date and time" ([Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/products/tasks/)). Things, Microsoft To Do, Apple Reminders and Any.do all treat the reminder as a deliberate per-task choice. Microsoft keeps "Remind me" and "Add due date" as separate fields ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/todo/add-due-dates-and-reminders-in-microsoft-to-do)).

Things is the model most relevant to Presense. **"A reminder is set via When. It reminds you to start a particular task,"** and deadlines can't carry a reminder at all. Cultured Code adds: "We suggest using reminders sparingly. Keep in mind that each reminder will interrupt what you're currently working on" ([Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803585/)). Splitting "when I'll start" (which can carry a reminder) from "when it's due" (which never does) fits an app built for people who struggle to start. A deadline reminder is a pressure signal. A start reminder is an invitation.

Date-only tasks are where apps differ most. **Apple fires all-day reminders at 9:00 AM by default, and users can change that time** ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102484)). Todoist's automatic reminders need a time, so date-only tasks stay silent ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/introduction-to-reminders-9PezfU)). Google announced 9 AM notifications for dated tasks in 2019, but its current help text and a June 2026 third-party guide suggest date-only tasks are now silent, so that behaviour is unsettled ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2019/03/set-start-times-and-import-reminders-in.html); [TasksBoard](https://tasksboard.com/blog/google-tasks-reminders)). Things moves start-dated items into the Today list and optionally shows a badge, with no push ([Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803579/)). Any.do sends one "Daily push" at a chosen time (9 AM by default) that opens its planning flow rather than listing every task ([Any.do](https://support.any.do/en/articles/8610107-how-notifications-work-permissions-sounds-daily-push-snooze)). In short, 9 AM is the industry default for "date but no time", and the better-designed apps fold date-only tasks into one daily prompt instead of firing a push per task.

Paywalls tell you what vendors think is premium. Todoist's free plan gives one reminder at the due time, while Pro adds multiple and "before task" reminders ([Todoist pricing](https://www.todoist.com/pricing)). Any.do charges for location and advanced recurring reminders ([Any.do](https://support.any.do/en/articles/12812921-notifications-reminders-overview)). A single reminder at a chosen time is the baseline everyone gives away.

## Snooze is standard; nagging is a separate, opt-in product

Snooze presets settle on **15 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours and tomorrow**. Things uses exactly that set, and Any.do adds Custom ([Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803585/); [Any.do](https://support.any.do/en/articles/8610107-how-notifications-work-permissions-sounds-daily-push-snooze)). Notification actions are near-universally "Done" plus "Snooze". TickTick and Any.do add a "Start Focus" button that starts a focus session straight from the alert ([TickTick](https://help.ticktick.com/articles/7055782395743567872)), which matters for an app whose job is getting started. Todoist offers snooze only on mobile, and users complain about the missing web and desktop snooze ([r/todoist](https://www.reddit.com/r/todoist/comments/1rjtfak/quick_reminder_snooze/)). The one large field study of snoozing, a year-long Android study with 295 active users, found people mostly deferred to **later the same day or the next morning, with targets driven by their daily routines**. Snoozing past two days was rare ([Weber et al. 2018](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Weber-MobileHCI18.pdf)). Snooze targets therefore work best as routine anchors ("this evening", "tomorrow morning"), not only as minute counts. No study links snooze count to eventual completion, so whether snoozing helps people get things done is unknown.

Persistence is where the ADHD-focused apps differ. **Due repeats an ignored reminder every 1, 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, five times by default and up to ten, until you complete or reschedule it** ([Due, App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/due-%E5%BF%AB%E9%80%9F%E6%8F%90%E9%86%92-%E5%AE%9A%E6%97%B6%E5%99%A8/id390017969?l=ar)). The reminder isn't finished when it's delivered. It stays live until the user makes a decision. A Boing Boing writer who used to forget reminders the moment they appeared says that "never happens anymore" ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2026/04/29/due-is-a-reminder-app-that-really-works.html)). TickTick's Constant Reminder "keeps notifying you until you take action". On Android it cycles 30 seconds on and 30 seconds off for five minutes ([TickTick](https://help.ticktick.com/articles/7374017550633402368)), and some users call the alarm mode "quite annoying" ([r/ticktick](https://www.reddit.com/r/ticktick/comments/1uhxu6f/how_to_use_normal_notifications_instead_of/)). Llama Life offers an "Audio: Nag" end-of-timer alarm alongside gentle interval chimes ([Llama Life](https://intercom.help/llama-life/en/articles/6456183-using-alarms)). Things deliberately refuses: "Reminders are not alarms. They won't keep 'ringing'" ([Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/support/articles/2803585/)).

The 2025-26 shift is **iOS 26 AlarmKit**, which lets apps fire full-screen alarms that break through Silent and Focus ([Apple AlarmKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/alarmkit.md)). Apple Reminders added "urgent" alarms in iOS 26.2, and they ring only on the device Find My uses for your location, so only one device rings ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102484)). Todoist's urgent reminders "cut through silent and Do Not Disturb" behind a one-time in-app explainer followed by the iOS alarm permission ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/add-an-urgent-reminder-in-todoist-WeBYdY5ra)). In 2025 Structured told users that Apple's rules had blocked critical alerts until iOS 26 ([Structured feedback](https://feedback.structured.app/p/critical-alerts-if-start-of-task-is-ignored-for-30)). Every one of these is a per-reminder opt-in behind a second OS permission, and **none of it is available to a web app**. Native iOS planners also use Live Activities to keep the current block visible without re-notifying ([Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/es/resource-hub/sensory-design-neurodivergent-accessibility)), which is also native-only.

The time-blocking planners for ADHD (Structured, Tiimo) alert at the **start and end** of a block by default. Structured also alerts 5 minutes before on Apple devices ([Structured](https://help.structured.app/en/articles/330434)), and Tiimo adds a halfway check-in ([AlternativeTo: Tiimo](https://alternativeto.net/software/tiimo/about/)). The end alert works as a transition cue. These defaults suit apps whose core object is a timed block. Presense's core object is a task someone is avoiding, so the pattern doesn't transfer directly.

## One predictable prompt replaces dozens of pings, and overdue tasks never re-notify

No official doc from any mainstream app describes an "overdue" push. **Todoist handles overdue tasks with an Overdue section and a Reschedule action** (also on its Android widget) ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/plan-your-day-with-the-today-view-UVUXaiSs)). Microsoft groups past items under "Earlier" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/todo/add-due-dates-and-reminders-in-microsoft-to-do)). Sunsama quietly rolls unfinished tasks to the next day ([Sunsama](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/getting-started/basics/task-rollover-and-recurring-tasks-the-basics)), an approach its founder describes as calm rather than frantic ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/sunsama-featured-tool)).

The ritual planners show the alternative to per-task pings. **Sunsama's shutdown prompt is a single in-app notification at a time the user picks during morning planning**, and it opens the shutdown ritual ([Sunsama changelog](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/changelog/daily-shutdown)). Sunsama users still ask for these prompts on mobile, and some call the lack of push a deal-breaker ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/mobile-apps/p/notifications-on-mobile-app)). That suggests ritual prompts only work if they reliably reach the phone. Presense is in the same position: its rituals exist, but its notification only fires while a tab is open.

The best experimental evidence supports this design. In a randomized two-week field experiment (n=237), **batching phone notifications to three times a day reduced stress and improved well-being, sense of control and concentration. Hourly batching did no better than the control, and turning notifications off entirely raised anxiety and fear of missing out** ([Fitz et al. 2019](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953)). That study batched *all* of a phone's notifications, so applying it to one app's reminders is an extrapolation. Even so, "a few predictable deliveries, at times the user chose" is the best-supported design available. On quiet hours, none of the planners examined document their own. Standard notifications follow the OS's Focus or Do Not Disturb, and only the alarm modes bypass it ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102484)). If every reminder fires at a time the user set, a separate quiet-hours setting adds little.

On tone, the evidence is indirect but points one way. Students who forgave themselves for procrastinating before a first exam procrastinated less before the next one (n=119) ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/cure-procrastination-forgive-yourself)). Across four samples (n=768), self-compassion was moderately and negatively associated with procrastination ([Sirois 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/91791/1/Procrastination%20and%20self%20compassion%20rev2%20FINAL.pdf)). No peer-reviewed experiment tests overdue badges or streak loss directly. The case against red counts and "you missed X" copy rests on mechanism, not on a measured effect. Habit-formation research adds that missing one opportunity "did not materially affect" reaching automaticity ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)), which undercuts streak punishment.

## The evidence on fatigue and ADHD supports restraint but can't settle the design

**Notifications themselves cause inattention.** In a within-subjects field experiment, 221 students reported more inattention and hyperactivity symptoms in a week with alerts on than in a week with alerts off ([Kushlev et al. 2016](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-CHI16.pdf)). People already receive around **63.5 notifications a day**, and more notifications came with more negative emotion ([Pielot et al. 2014](https://ic.unicamp.br/~oliveira/doc/MHCI2014_An-in-situ-study-of-mobile-phone-notifications.pdf), n=15, small). Important content doesn't protect a notification from feeling disruptive ([Mehrotra et al. 2016](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1502364/1/CHI16.pdf)). Airship's 2026 benchmarks name "too frequently" as the top reason people opt out ([Airship](https://www.airship.com/mobile-app-push-notification-benchmarks-for-2026/)). Airship is a vendor and its data is correlational. For a product aimed at people with ADHD, the uncomfortable implication is that a noisy reminder system can make worse the very symptom it is meant to offset.

What helps people start is better established than what reminds them. **Implementation intentions ("when X happens, I will do Y") improved goal attainment with an average d of 0.65 across 94 tests** ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)). Interruptions at natural breakpoints cost less than interruptions mid-task ([Bailey & Iqbal 2008](https://interruptions.net/literature/Bailey-TOCHI08.pdf)), and messages delivered at activity transitions were rated better than ones at random times ([Ho & Intille 2005](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Ho-CHI05-p909-ho.pdf)). Barkley's clinical principle of putting cues at the "point of performance" argues that a reminder should open the task itself, not a list ([Shortform summary](https://www.shortform.com/blog/how-to-manage-adhd-as-an-adult/); secondary source). In the habit research, reminders increased how often people repeated a behaviour but **hindered automaticity**, while event-based cues built it ([Stawarz et al. 2015](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/)). For ADHD, though, lasting external scaffolding may be the goal rather than independence, and no study reconciles the two views.

Several gaps should temper confidence. **No randomized trial isolates the effect of push reminders on task starting in adults with ADHD.** The closest, MyADHD-digital, finished in 2021, but its results weren't retrieved ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04511169)). The popular claim that ADHD users stop noticing a repeated alert "within weeks or sometimes days" comes from vendor blog content, not research ([yougot.ai](https://www.yougot.ai/blog/neurodivergent/adhd-tools/why-do-reminders-not-work-for-adhd)). Due's following among ADHD users is anecdotal. Nothing measures habituation to repeated reminders for the same task over several days. The steelman for nagging is real: decision-forcing persistence is what Due users credit, and it can't be ruled out as the most effective mechanism for some users. The evidence simply can't show whether it helps or harms on average.

## Web Push in 2026 works everywhere, but only from the server and only after a click

**Scheduled local notifications on the web are dead.** Chrome ended Notification Triggers because "it wasn't clear that we could provide consistent and reliable experiences across platforms" ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/notification-triggers)). Periodic Background Sync is Chromium-only, requires an installed app, depends on site engagement and gives no timing guarantee ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/periodic-background-sync?hl=it)). A reminder that has to fire at 3:00 PM with the app closed therefore needs a **server push sent at 3:00 PM**. In-page timers only work while a tab is open. Presense's current ritual notification has a second problem: on Android Chrome, **the `Notification` constructor "always throws a TypeError"**, so it has to be replaced with `registration.showNotification()` from the service worker ([MDN BCD](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/api/Notification.json)).

Every engine now enforces permission rules. Firefox (since version 72) and Safari only allow `requestPermission()` and `pushManager.subscribe()` inside a user gesture ([Mozilla Hacks](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2019/11/upcoming-notification-permission-changes-in-firefox-72/)). Chrome puts sites with low acceptance rates behind a quieter permission UI ([Chromium blog](https://blog.chromium.org/2020/01/introducing-quieter-permission-ui-for.html)) and recommends in-context toggles over prompts on landing ([Chrome developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/notification-permission-data-in-crux/)). Since October 2025 Chrome also **auto-revokes notification permission from sites with low engagement and high volume, except installed web apps** ([Google](https://blog.google/chromium/automatic-notification-permission/)). Presense requests permission from a timer, which fails outright on Firefox and Safari and counts as the kind of cold prompt Chrome penalises.

iPhone is the hard target. **Web Push works on iOS only for Home Screen web apps**. iOS 26 opens every Home Screen site as a web app, but there is still no install prompt ([Monterail](https://monterail.com/blog/pwa-for-apple-ios); [webscraft](https://webscraft.org/blog/pwa-pushspovischennya-na-ios-u-2026-scho-realno-pratsyuye?lang=en)). The EU removal was reversed in March 2024 ([GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/apple_backtracks_wont_remove_progressive_web_apps_in_the_eu_after_all-news-61836.php)). **Safari supports no notification action buttons**, and also no `requireInteraction` or `renotify` ([MDN BCD](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/api/Notification.json)), so "Done" and "Snooze" have to live on the screen the notification opens. A classic push that doesn't display a notification gets the permission revoked ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)). Subscriptions sometimes disappear silently, `pushsubscriptionchange` isn't available to recover them ([WebKit bug 273063](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=273063)), and people report pushes stopping after iOS 18.4 ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/848080)). One blog's 70-85% iOS delivery estimate has no stated methodology and shouldn't be relied on. **Declarative Web Push** (iOS 18.4, Safari 18.5) helps here. The server sends JSON with `"web_push": 8030`, a `title` and a `navigate` URL, and the OS shows it even if the service worker fails, with no penalty ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/16535/meet-declarative-web-push/)). Chrome and Firefox still need the service worker to parse that same payload. Chrome, Edge and Firefox 152+ do show action buttons and fire `notificationclick` with `event.action` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/ServiceWorkerRegistration/showNotification)).

The server side is standard but has sharp edges. RFC 8030 requires a `TTL` header (requests without it get a 400). `Urgency: high` marks a message as time-sensitive and lets it through on low battery. A `Topic` of up to 32 base64url characters replaces an *undelivered* earlier message, and payloads up to 4096 bytes must be accepted ([RFC 8030](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8030.html)). Conveniently, a UUID with its dashes removed is exactly 32 valid characters. **`jsr:@negrel/webpush` (v0.5.0) implements VAPID and payload encryption on Web Crypto and runs on Deno**, but its maintainer notes it hasn't had a cryptographic review ([JSR](https://jsr.io/@negrel/webpush)). No source confirmed that `npm:web-push` works in Supabase Edge Functions. Supabase Cron supports sub-minute schedules ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/extensions/pgcron)), but Supabase has no official VAPID example; its push guide covers only Expo and FCM ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/functions/examples/push-notifications)).

## What Presense should build, in three phases

Taken together, this points to the Things model, delivered over Web Push. Reminders are opt-in per task and attached to when the user intends to start. Date-only tasks get no per-task push and surface through the morning ritual instead. There's no nagging and no overdue alert. Two daily ritual prompts sit at times the user chose, matching the three-a-day batching evidence. Every notification opens one task, ready to start.

**Phase 0: quick fixes (days, no new infrastructure).** Replace `new Notification()` with `navigator.serviceWorker.ready.then(r => r.showNotification(...))`. This alone stops the Android crash. Move the permission request off the timer and onto an explicit "Remind me at my planning time" button in settings or ritual setup, with a short in-app explanation first. Call `Notification.requestPermission()` synchronously inside the click handler. Add a `notificationclick` handler to the Serwist service worker that focuses an existing window or opens the ritual route. On iOS Safari outside standalone mode, show an "Add to Home Screen" explainer instead of a button that can't work. Delete `notification_sent_72h/24h/6h/1h/overdue`, `notif_overdue` and `notif_stale_threads` in a migration. A 72/24/6/1-hour deadline countdown is exactly the escalating, deadline-anchored nag the leading calm apps avoid, overdue pushes appear in no mainstream app's docs, and stale-thread pushes contradict the owner's "never push for stuck tasks" rule. Dead columns invite someone to wire them up later. Be clear that Phase 0 still only fires while a tab is open, so it fixes the crash but not reliability.

**Phase 1: server-sent reminders via Web Push (the real build).** Add a `push_subscriptions` table (user, unique `endpoint`, keys, user agent, `last_seen_at`) and a `reminders` table (task id, user, `remind_at timestamptz`, `kind` of `task` or `ritual_morning` or `ritual_evening`, `sent_at`). Ritual reminders are generated from the user's chosen times and IANA time zone. Per-task reminders exist only when the user taps "Remind me" on a task and picks a time, defaulting to the task's start time if it has one. **Never create a reminder automatically from `deadline`.** Date-only deadlines stored as 23:59 must never produce a push, because a reminder at one minute to midnight is useless and stressful. Schedule a pg_cron job every 30-60 seconds that calls a Deno Edge Function. The function claims due rows with `UPDATE … SET sent_at = now() WHERE id IN (SELECT … WHERE sent_at IS NULL AND remind_at <= now() AND remind_at > now() - interval '15 minutes' FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED LIMIT 500) RETURNING …`, then sends to every subscription the user has. Send a declarative-format payload with `TTL` around 15-30 minutes, `Urgency: high`, `Topic` set to the task UUID without dashes, and notification `tag` set to `task-<id>`. Delete subscriptions that return 404 or 410. Check `net._http_response`, not `cron.job_run_details`, the lesson the existing cron jobs already taught. On every app open, call `getSubscription()` and re-subscribe or upsert if needed, and show a "Reminders work on this device" status with a test button. The notification opens `/do?task=<id>&from=reminder`. That screen leads with the task and its smallest first step, a "Start" control, and snooze options of **"In an hour", "This evening" and "Tomorrow morning"**, which follow the routine-anchored deferral pattern found in the snooze study. Snoozing writes `snoozed_until` and a new reminder row.

Use `defer_count` only inside the app. After roughly three snoozes of the same task, the landing screen offers to shrink it to a smaller first step. It never sends a push about it. Copy names the task and the first step, in a neutral, forward-looking voice ("Ready when you are: open the draft"). It never counts overdue items or mentions misses. The 15-minute claim window, the TTL and the three-snooze threshold are design judgements, not findings from the research.

**Phase 2: optional refinements, only if usage justifies them.** Add Chromium and Firefox action buttons ("Start", "Later today"). The service worker has no Supabase session, so these need a short-lived signed action token in the push data, verified by a route handler. Safari users would still act in-app. Close a task's notification on the user's other devices when it's completed, via a second push and `getNotifications({tag})`, but skip `*.push.apple.com` endpoints, because a classic push that shows nothing risks revocation there. Let users phrase a reminder as an anchor ("after lunch") restated in the copy, which draws on the implementation-intention evidence. Don't build nag or repeat-until-done modes. Web Push can't match native alarms anyway, the owner rejects nagging, and the evidence for it is anecdotal. If users ask for persistence, the most that fits is a single opt-in follow-up per reminder. Don't build quiet hours either, since every push fires at a user-chosen time. Don't add badges for overdue counts.

| Area | Presense recommendation | Evidence strength |
|---|---|---|
| Per-task default | Off; opt-in "Remind me", tied to start time, never to deadline | Strong industry convention (Things, Apple, Microsoft); no outcome data |
| Date-only tasks | No per-task push; surface in the morning ritual | Convention (Things, Any.do); batching RCT is an extrapolation |
| Ritual prompts | Two server pushes at user-chosen times | Moderate (Fitz 2019, all-phone batching) |
| Overdue / stuck | Visual reschedule only; no push | Convention plus indirect self-compassion evidence |
| Snooze | Routine-anchored presets, handled in-app | Moderate (Weber 2018, n=295); no completion data |
| Nagging | Don't build | Weak either way; anecdotal user praise vs. Kushlev's inattention effect |
| Permission | Gesture-triggered, in context, after explanation | Strong (browser rules enforce it) |
| Delivery | Server Web Push + Declarative payload; re-subscribe on open | Strong on mechanism; iOS reliability poorly quantified |

## Conclusion

The striking finding is that the most respected apps compete on **restraint, not reach**. The industry has moved persistence into explicit, separately permitted alarm modes that a PWA can't build, while the calm default stays one reminder, chosen by the user, aimed at starting. For Presense the platform limit is useful: without notification buttons on iPhone and without native alarms, every reminder has to bring the user back into the app, onto a screen built to make the first step easy. That screen, not the notification, is where Presense can do better than its competitors.

The open question is empirical, and Presense is in a position to answer it. Nobody has measured whether start-oriented reminders actually shorten the time to start a task for people with ADHD, or whether repeated snoozes predict abandonment. Logging, privately and per user, whether a reminder tap leads to a started task, and how `defer_count` evolves afterwards, would turn Presense's design from borrowed convention into evidence of its own.
